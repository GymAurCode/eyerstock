## 1. What this app is for

Minha Inventory runs the whole shop from one computer: selling, buying, stock,
money owed, service visits, warranty and the tax return. It never connects to
the internet. Nothing you type leaves this machine.

The app works on **one computer only**. There is no cloud copy and no second
till connected to the same data. That is why the app shows this message on the
dashboard:

> This app stores everything on this one computer. Back up to a USB stick or
> OneDrive — there is no cloud copy.

### The golden rule

**Back up regularly, to somewhere outside this computer.**

If the hard disk fails, the copies inside the app folder go with it. A copy on a
USB stick or in OneDrive does not. Section [16. Backing up](#16-backing-up)
explains how to set this up. It takes two minutes and it is the single most
important thing you can do.

## 2. Setting up for the first time

The first time the app opens, it shows the **Set up Minha Inventory** screen.
Fill it in once, in this order.

### 2.1 Company details

Under **Company details**:

1. **Company name** — this prints on every invoice. Use the legal name.
2. **TRN (tax number)** — your tax registration number. It prints on every tax
   invoice, so get it right.
3. **Company phone** — the number customers should call.
4. **Company address** — printed on invoices.
5. **Financial year starts in** — the month your financial year begins.
6. **Language** — choose English or العربية. You can change this later.

### 2.2 Your administrator account

Under **Create administrator account**:

1. **Your name** — the name that appears on the audit log and on documents you
   create. Use your real name, not a nickname.
2. **Username** — what you type to sign in. Keep it short, with no spaces.
3. **Password** — the rule is shown under the box: *Use at least 8 characters,
   with a capital letter and a number.*

4. Click **Finish setup**.

You are now signed in as the administrator. This account can do everything,
including creating other users and changing who may see what.

### 2.3 The setup checklist

At the bottom of the **Dashboard** there is a **Getting started** list. Each line
is a step you can tick off with the **done** button as you go, or hide the whole
list with **Dismiss**. It disappears once every step is done, and the keyboard
shortcut reminder takes its place.

## 3. Signing in and signing out

### 3.1 Signing in

1. Open **Minha Inventory** from the desktop.
2. Type your **Username**.
3. Type your **Password**.
4. Click **Sign in**.

If either is wrong the app says **Wrong username or password.** and you can try
again. It will not tell you which one was wrong.

### 3.2 If you are locked out

After **5** wrong attempts in a row, the account is locked for **15 minutes**.
This protects your data if someone else tries to use your sign-in.

- Wait 15 minutes and try again, or
- Ask an administrator to reset your password for you (see
  [19. If something goes wrong](#19-if-something-goes-wrong)).

A correct password clears the counter. The app warns you when only a couple of
attempts are left.

### 3.3 Signing out

Click the **Sign out** button at the bottom of the left menu, or the round
avatar at the top right and then **Sign out**.

### 3.4 Changing your own password

**Administration → Settings**, scroll to **Change password**. Enter
**Current password**, **New password** and **Confirm password**, then click
**Change password**.

If an administrator reset your password for you, the app asks you to set a new
one before you can continue.

## 4. How the screen is laid out

### 4.1 The left menu

The menu on the left is in six groups. You only see the groups your role is
allowed to open, so your menu may look shorter than this list.

| Group | What is in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Overview** | **Dashboard** |
| **Sales & Purchases** | **Invoices**, **Quotations**, **Purchases**, **Returns & credit notes**, **Payments**, **Post-dated cheques** |
| **Inventory** | **Products**, **Stock**, **Kits & bundles**, **Barcode labels**, **Customers**, **Suppliers** |
| **Finance** | **Expenses**, **VAT report**, **Partners**, **Period lock**, **Reports** |
| **Service** | **Service contracts**, **Filter schedule**, **Warranty**, **Installations** |
| **Administration** | **Users**, **Roles & permissions**, **Audit log**, **Backup & restore**, **Import products**, **Licence key**, **Settings**, **About** |

Click **Collapse menu** at the bottom to make the menu narrow and show icons
only. Click it again to widen it. The app remembers your choice.

If you reach a screen you are not allowed to open, the app shows
**You do not have access to this screen.** and *Ask an administrator to change
your role.* Tell them which screen it was; they can fix it in a few seconds.

### 4.2 The top bar

Along the top, from left to right:

- The **name of the screen** you are on, with the path back to the list above it.
- The **search box**, which says *Search customers, invoices, items…*.
- The **bell** — anything that needs attention today: low stock, services due
  this week, cheques due, warranties expiring. Click one to jump to it.
- The **language** button — English or العربية.
- The **moon or sun** — switches between light and dark.
- Your **avatar** — opens **Settings**, **About** and **Sign out**.

### 4.3 Searching

Press **Ctrl + K** from anywhere. A box opens that finds both screens and
actions. Type a few letters, move with the **up and down arrows**, press
**Enter** to open, **Esc** to close. It only offers things your role may do.

Every list also has its own search box above the table. Type any part of a name,
number or code and the list narrows as you type.

### 4.4 Keyboard shortcuts

| Keys | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Ctrl + K** | Open search and the command list |
| **Ctrl + N** | Start a **New invoice** |
| **Ctrl + P** | Start a **New purchase** |
| **Ctrl + S** | Save the invoice, purchase or quotation you are on |
| **Esc** | Close the search box or a dialog |
| **Enter** in **Quantity** | Jump to **Unit price** |
| **Enter** in **Unit price** | Add another line |

On a sales screen you never have to touch the mouse: pick the item, type the
quantity, press **Enter**, type the price, press **Enter** for the next line.

## 5. Customers and suppliers

### 5.1 Adding a customer

1. Go to **Inventory → Customers**.
2. Click **New customer**.
3. Fill in:

| Field | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Name** | Required. This is what prints on the invoice. |
| **Customer** | Leave as **Customer**. Change it to **Supplier** or **Both** if the business buys from and sells to them. |
| **Phone** | So you can call them. |
| **Email** | Optional. |
| **TRN** | Only if the business is VAT-registered. |
| **Address** | Optional. |
| **Emirate** | Choose from the list. |
| **Credit limit** | How much you are willing to let them owe. Informational. |
| **Payment terms (days)** | Used to work out when an invoice becomes overdue. |
| **Status** | **Active** or **Inactive**. |

4. Click **Save**.

A code such as `C-0001` is created for you. You do not choose it.

### 5.2 Adding a supplier

**Inventory → Suppliers**, then **New supplier**. The same form, the same steps.
For a supplier, the **Balance** means what **you** owe them.

### 5.3 Opening a statement — who owes what

1. Go to **Inventory → Customers**.
2. Click the customer's name, or click **Statement** in the row menu.

The **Statement** screen shows:

- **Aging** — four boxes: **Not due**, **1-30 days**, **31-60 days** and
  **Over 60 days**, with a count of **Overdue** invoices at the top.
- The full **ledger**: every **Date**, **Invoice number**, **Cost**, **Paid** and
  running **Balance**, ending in **Closing balance**.

3. Click **Print** to give the customer a copy.
4. Click **Close** when you are done.

### 5.4 Taking a payment from a customer

From the same row menu, **Receive payment** takes you straight to that customer
on the payment screen. See [10. Taking and making payments](#10-taking-and-making-payments).

### 5.5 Editing or stopping trading with someone

Click **Edit** in the row menu, change what you need and click **Save**. To stop
trading with someone, set **Status** to **Inactive** rather than deleting them —
their invoices and payments stay exactly where they are.

## 6. Products and stock

### 6.1 Adding an item

1. Go to **Inventory → Products**.
2. Click **New item**.
3. Fill in:

| Field | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Item name** | Required. Prints on the invoice. |
| **Item name (Arabic)** | Optional. Used when the app is in Arabic. |
| **Category** | Any word you like, for example *Filters* or *Pumps*. |
| **Item code** | Your own short code, such as `RO-5STAGE`. Leave it blank and one is generated. |
| **Barcode** | The number your scanner will read. Optional. |
| **Unit** | **pcs**, **litre**, **metre**, **box**, **set** or **hour**. |
| **Sale price** | What you charge. |
| **Reorder level** | The number at which the item shows as low. |
| **VAT treatment** | See below. |
| **Warranty (months)** | Used to work out warranty end dates. |
| **Track serial numbers** | Tick for pumps and RO units sold one at a time. |
| **Service (no stock)** | Tick for labour, installation or AMC fees. These do not reduce stock. |
| **Status** | **Active** or **Inactive**. |

4. Click **Save**.

**Cost is not typed here.** The app works it out from your purchases, so the
average cost and your profit stay correct even when prices change.

### 6.2 VAT treatment — including **Outside scope**

The **VAT treatment** field decides how tax is calculated on this item, both on
sales and on the VAT report.

| Choice | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| **Standard 5%** | Ordinary sales. The normal choice. |
| **Zero rated** | Sales that are zero-rated by law. |
| **Exempt** | Sales that are exempt. |
| **Outside scope** | Something that is outside the scope of VAT altogether — for example certain financial or insurance services. It never appears as output or input VAT. |

On any invoice line you can override the item's setting using the **VAT** box on
that line, which offers **Standard 5%**, **Zero rated** and **Exempt**. You
cannot put **Outside scope** on a single line; set it on the item instead.

If you are unsure, ask your accountant before changing it.

### 6.3 Setting your opening stock

When you start using the app you already have stock on the shelf. Put it in once:

1. Go to **Inventory → Products**.
2. Click the **⋮** menu at the right of the item and choose **Set stock**.
3. Enter the **Opening stock** — the quantity you counted.
4. Enter the **Cost** you paid for it.
5. Put a note such as *Stock count* in **Notes**.
6. Click **Save**.

The app adds a stock movement so there is a record of what you did.

### 6.4 Correcting stock later

If you count the shelf and it does not match, do not change the number on the
item. Use **Set stock** again and enter the counted quantity with a **Notes**
line saying what happened. The app keeps both movements, so you can always show
why the number changed.

### 6.5 Seeing stock warnings

- **Products** shows a red **On hand** figure and a **Low stock items** badge for
  anything at or below its **Reorder level**.
- Tick **Low stock only** above the table to see just those.
- The **Dashboard** shows a **Low stock items** tile. Click it to open the list.
- The bell in the top bar also warns you.

## 7. Selling something

### 7.1 Creating an invoice quickly

Press **Ctrl + N**, or go to **Sales & Purchases → Invoices** and click
**New invoice**.

1. Click the **Customer** box and start typing a name. Pick them from the list.
2. Press **Enter** or click into the first line.
3. Click the **Item** box and type the name or the item code. Pick it.
4. Type the **Quantity**, press **Enter**, type the **Unit price**, press
   **Enter**.
5. Repeat for every item, or click **Add line** to add a blank one.

The price and VAT fill in by themselves. The **Subtotal**, **VAT** and **Total**
update as you type.

Add anything the driver should know in **Notes**. Set a **Due date** if the
customer pays later.

6. Click **Save and issue**.

A blue message says **Invoice saved.** and the invoice opens, ready to print.

### 7.2 Scanning a barcode

If you have a USB barcode scanner, you do not need to click anything.

1. Plug the scanner into the computer.
2. Open a **New invoice**.
3. Point it at the barcode.

A strip across the item lines says *Scanner ready — scan a barcode to add the
product.* Scan the code and the item fills into the next empty line, with its
price and VAT, and the cursor jumps to **Quantity**.

If a code is not recognised, a red message shows **Nothing to show** and the
code. Check the **Barcode** on that item under **Products**.

### 7.3 Selling a kit

If the shop sells pre-built RO kits:

1. In the **Lines** panel, use the **Kits** box.
2. Choose **Add kit** and pick the kit from the list.
3. The kit's parts appear as separate lines.

The customer is charged the **Kit price** in total; the parts are shared out
across the lines by cost. Stock comes off **each part**, so the shelf counts
stay right. If a part is short you will see *Some parts of this kit are short on
stock* — record a purchase or reduce the quantity before issuing.

### 7.4 Saving as a draft versus issuing

At the bottom of the editor there are two buttons:

- **Save as draft** — keeps the invoice but does **not** move stock, does not
  count for VAT and does not appear on anybody's balance. Use it while you are
  still working out the details.
- **Save and issue** — makes the invoice real: stock comes off, the VAT report
  counts it and the customer's balance grows.

You can still edit a draft from **Invoices**, using **Edit** in the row menu.

### 7.5 Printing the invoice

On the invoice screen:

- **Print** sends it to your printer.
- **Save as PDF** saves a copy to your computer.

Both print your company name, TRN, logo, the lines, VAT, total and the footer
and terms from **Administration → Settings**.

### 7.6 Giving a discount

Type a lower **Unit price** on the line before you save. The VAT is recalculated
on the new price.

### 7.7 Serial numbers

If the item has **Track serial numbers** ticked, a small box appears under the
item name on the line. Enter the serial numbers separated by commas, for example
`RO-24115, RO-24116`. These are what **Warranty** looks up later.

### 7.8 Cancelling or deleting

- **Cancel document** reverses the sale: stock comes back and the VAT is
  reversed. You must give a **Reason for cancelling**. A cancelled document keeps
  its number and stays on record — this is what the tax authority expects.
- **Delete** only appears on a **Draft**. Deleting a draft can be taken back: a
  message appears in the bottom-right corner with an **Undo** button. You have a
  few seconds to press it. The restored draft gets a new number, because the old
  one is gone.

An issued invoice can never be deleted. Cancel it instead.

## 8. Quotations, delivery notes and returns

### 8.1 Quoting

1. Go to **Sales & Purchases → Quotations**.
2. Click **New quotation**.
3. Add the customer and the lines, exactly as on an invoice.
4. Click **Save as draft** or **Save and issue**.

A quotation does not move stock and does not create a debt.

### 8.2 Turning a quotation into an invoice

1. Go to **Sales & Purchases → Quotations**.
2. Click the **⋮** menu on the quotation and choose **Convert to invoice**.
3. Confirm with **Convert to invoice**.

The invoice opens with the same lines and a new invoice number. The quotation
stays on record, marked as converted, so you cannot convert it twice.

### 8.3 Delivery notes

A delivery note is a document that travels with the goods and carries no money.

1. Go to **Sales & Purchases → Invoices**.
2. Click the **⋮** menu on the invoice and choose **Create delivery note**.

The note is made from the invoice, so the lines are already filled in. It gets
its own document number, carries no money and no VAT, and is kept on record as
coming from that invoice. The app will tell you if a delivery note already exists
for that invoice.

### 8.4 Credit notes and returns

If a customer brings something back:

1. Go to **Sales & Purchases → Returns & credit notes**.
2. Click **Raise credit note**.
3. Choose the **Customer**, then click the invoice they are returning against.
4. The lines are filled in from that invoice. Reduce the **Quantity** on the
   lines being returned, and put the reason in **Reason** — for example
   *Damaged*.
5. Click the right-hand button. On a credit note it reads **Credit note saved.**
   The app then confirms **Credit note saved.** in the corner and returns you to
   the list.

You cannot return more than was still returnable on that invoice. Stock goes
back up at the price you originally paid, not today's price, so your profit
figures stay right. The customer's balance goes down.

Credit note numbers are never skipped, because the tax authority expects an
unbroken sequence.

### 8.5 Cancelling a quotation, purchase or credit note

1. Open its list (**Quotations**, **Purchases** or **Returns & credit notes**).
2. Click the row, or choose **Statement** in the **⋮** menu.
3. Click **Cancel document** and give a reason, in the same way as an invoice.

## 9. Recording a purchase

### 9.1 Entering a purchase

Press **Ctrl + P**, or go to **Sales & Purchases → Purchases** and click
**New purchase**.

1. Choose the **Supplier**.
2. Add the lines: pick the **Item**, set the **Quantity** and the price you
   paid.
3. Set the **Issue date** to the date on the supplier's invoice, and a
   **Due date** if you pay later.
4. Click **Save and issue**.

As soon as you issue it:

- **Stock** increases by the quantities entered.
- The **average cost** of each item is recalculated, which is what keeps your
  profit figure correct.
- The amount appears as **Money you owe** on the **Dashboard**.

If you do not record purchases, your profit will look far higher than it really
is.

### 9.2 Checking what was bought

**Sales & Purchases → Purchases** lists everything, with a search box and a
**Status** filter. Open any purchase to see the lines, then **Print** it.

## 10. Taking and making payments

### 10.1 Receiving money from a customer

1. Go to **Sales & Purchases → Payments**.
2. Click **Receive payment**.

Or go straight from an unpaid invoice: open the invoice and click **Receive
payment**. The customer and that invoice are filled in for you.

3. Under **Received from**, pick the customer.
4. Set the **Payment date**.
5. Choose the **Method**: **Cash**, **Bank transfer**, **Cheque**, **Card** or
   **Online payment**.
6. Enter the **Amount**.
7. Put the bank reference or cheque number in **Reference** if you have one.

Then use the **Open invoices** panel to say which invoices the money settles:

- Click **Allocate oldest first** and the app spreads the amount over the
  oldest unpaid invoices first.
- Or click **Add allocation** and choose the invoices yourself, then type an
  amount against each.
- Anything left over is held as **On account** — a credit on their account, not
  lost money.

8. Click **Save**.

A receipt prints and you are returned to **Payment history**.

### 10.2 Making a partial payment

This is the same screen. Enter an amount that is less than what the customer
owes, allocate it to one or more invoices, and click **Save**. The invoices
show **Part paid** and the balance remains.

### 10.3 Paying a supplier

Click **Make payment** on the **Payments** screen. Everything is the same except
the heading is **Paid to** and you pick a supplier.

### 10.4 Looking at payment history

**Sales & Purchases → Payments** shows every payment, incoming and outgoing.
Filter by **Received from** or **Paid to**, or by **From** and **To** dates.
Click any row to see what it was allocated against, or **Print** the receipt.
**Delete payment** removes a payment and puts the balances back, if your role
allows it.

## 11. Post-dated cheques

A customer hands you a cheque dated for next month. Record it so you know when
to chase it.

### 11.1 Recording a cheque

1. Go to **Finance → Post-dated cheques**.
2. Click **Add**.
3. Fill in:

| Field | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Method** | **Received from** for a customer, **Paid to** for a cheque you gave a supplier. |
| **Customer** | Who gave or holds the cheque. |
| **Cheque number** | Required. Exactly as printed. |
| **Bank** | Required. The bank name. |
| **Amount** | The value of the cheque. |
| **Issue date** | The date written on the cheque. |
| **Due on** | The date it clears. |

4. Click **Save**.

### 11.2 Seeing what is due

- The **Dashboard** tile **Cheques due** counts cheques falling due.
- The bell in the top bar warns you.
- **Post-dated cheques** has filters for **Status** and for **Due this week**,
  **Due this month** or 90 days.

### 11.3 When it clears or bounces

On the cheque row:

- Click the green tick, **Mark cleared**, then **Confirm**. The app records
  today's date as the clearing date.
- Click the red circle, **Mark bounced**, give a **Why was it bounced?**, then
  **Confirm**.

## 12. Expenses, VAT and reports

### 12.1 Recording an expense

An expense is money spent that is **not** stock — rent, fuel, salaries. It does
not create a debt to anyone.

1. Go to **Finance → Expenses**.
2. Click **New expense**.
3. Fill in **Date**, **Category**, **Description**, **Amount**, **VAT** (if
   any), **Paid by**, the **Supplier** if there is one, and **Reference**.
4. Click **Save**.

Categories are rent, utilities, transport, salaries, marketing, maintenance,
office, internet, deposit and general.

Expenses you forget to record make your profit look better than it is.

### 12.2 The VAT report — this is the one for your accountant

1. Go to **Finance → VAT report**.
2. Set the **From** and **To** dates for the tax period.
3. Read the four boxes across the top:

| Box | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Output VAT** | VAT you charged on sales. Shown with **Sales (net)** underneath. |
| **Input VAT** | VAT you paid on purchases. Shown with **Purchases (net)**. |
| **Credit notes** | VAT on credit notes raised in the period, with **Expenses**. |
| **Net payable** | Output minus input. This is what you owe the tax authority. |

4. Click **Export to Excel** to save the period as a spreadsheet for your
   accountant.
5. Click **Print** on the detail table to print the invoice list behind the
   summary.

**If Net payable is negative**, it means you paid more VAT than you collected
and are owed a credit. Report it as a negative figure. Do not change it to zero.

### 12.3 Reports

Go to **Finance → Reports**. There are three tabs.

**Profit and loss** — pick **From** and **To**, then read down the page:
**Revenue**, **Returns & credit notes**, **Net sales**, **Cost of goods sold**,
**Gross profit**, **Gross margin**, **Operating expenses** and **Net profit**.
Beside it is a bar chart of expenses by category.

**Aging** — two panels:

- **Money owed to you (Customer)**
- **Money you owe (Supplier)**

Each is split into **Not due**, **1-30 days**, **31-60 days** and
**Over 60 days**, with an **Overdue** total at the top. This is where you find
who to chase.

**Stock valuation** — every item with its **Item code**, **Item name**,
**On hand** and **Stock value**.

Click **Export data** at the top to save all of it as a spreadsheet.

## 13. Partners and closing the month

If two people share the business, the **Partners** screen settles the month.

### 13.1 Setting up the partners

1. Go to **Finance → Partners**.
2. Click **Add partner**.
3. Enter the **Name** and the **Share %**. For two equal partners this is 50
   and 50.
4. Click **Save**.

### 13.2 Capital and drawings

**Add entry** records money moving in or out of the business by a partner:

- **Type** = **Capital** when a partner puts money in.
- **Type** = **Drawings** when a partner takes money out for personal use.

Pick the **Partner**, the **Date** and the **Amount**, then click **Save**. Both
appear under **Capital & drawings**.

Drawings reduce what a partner ends the period with. Be clear with your partner
about these.

### 13.3 The closing statement

1. Go to **Finance → Partners**.
2. Set the **Financial year** and **Month**.
3. Read the **Closing statement**:

| Line | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Revenue** | Sales in the month. |
| **Cost of goods sold** | What the stock sold cost you. |
| **Operating expenses** | Everything from **Expenses**. |
| **Net profit** | What is left. |
| **Donation** | Taken off the top, 2% by default. |
| **Distributable profit** | What is left for the partners. |

Then a table with one row per partner showing **Share**, **Capital**,
**Drawings** and **Closing balance**, and a **Total** line.

For two partners at 50% each with the default donation rate, this is the
2% donation and the 49 / 49 split. The figures are worked out in whole fils, so
they add up exactly — there is no rounding gap to argue about.

Change the **Donation rate (%)** in the box above the statement if your
arrangement is different.

### 13.4 Closing the period

1. Check every figure on the statement.
2. Click **Print** if you want a copy for each partner.
3. Click **Close period**.
4. A box asks you to type **CLOSE** to confirm closing this period.
5. Type **CLOSE** and confirm.

Once closed, the period cannot be edited or backdated into.

### 13.5 Locking the period

Locking stops anyone typing into a closed month.

1. Go to **Finance → Period lock**.
2. Set the **Financial year**.
3. Find the month and click **Lock period**.

The card then says **Locked** and shows who locked it and when. Click
**Unlock period** to open it again.

Two things to remember:

- Close the period before locking it — the screen reminds you of this.
- In a locked period the app says *This period is locked. No entries can be
  added, changed or removed in it.*

## 14. Service and warranty

### 14.1 Service contracts

A contract is your annual maintenance agreement.

1. Go to **Service → Service contracts**.
2. Click **New service contract**.
3. Fill in the **Customer**, the **Item** fitted, the **Installed on** date, an
   end date under **Due on**, the **Service interval (days)** — how often you
   visit — and the **Visits included** in the contract, plus the **Amount**
   charged.
4. Set the **Status**: **Draft**, **Active**, **Expired** or **Cancelled**.
5. Click **Save**.

The list shows **Visits left** and **Next service due** on every row, so you can
sell a renewal before the visits run out.

### 14.2 Logging a visit

1. Click the **⋮** menu on the contract and choose **Log visit**. You can also
   click the row itself.
2. Enter the **Visit date**, the **Engineer** who attended, what was done in
   **Work done**, the **Parts cost**, and the **Next service due** date.
3. Click **Save**.

**Visits used** are listed underneath, so you always have the history. The
**Visits left** figure drops by one each time.

### 14.3 The filter schedule and what is due

**Service → Filter schedule** is your day planner.

- The list defaults to **Due this week**. Use **Due this month** for the wider
  view or **All** for everything outstanding.
- The **Dashboard** tile **Services due this week** takes you straight there.

For each row the **⋮** menu offers:

- **WhatsApp reminder** — opens WhatsApp with a message already written for that
  customer. You press send yourself; the app never sends anything on its own and
  never connects to the internet. The option is greyed out if the customer has
  no phone number on file.
- **Print reminder** — prints a slip for the customer to keep.
- **Mark done** — records that you changed the filter today.
- **Edit** — change the **Quantity**, the **Due on** date or the **Notes**.

You can also add a reminder yourself with **New reminder**: pick the
**Customer**, the **Item**, the **Quantity** and the **Due on** date.

### 14.4 Warranty lookup

When a customer calls about a pump:

1. Go to **Service → Warranty**.
2. Type the serial number, the customer's name, or part of their phone number
   in the box.
3. Click **Look up warranty**.

You are told whether it is **In warranty** or **Expired**, the **Warranty starts**
and **Warranty ends** dates, who bought it and **Sold on**.

Below the search box, **Warranties expiring** lists the next twenty units whose
warranty runs out, so you can call those customers before the claim window
closes.

### 14.5 Logging a warranty claim

On a unit you have looked up, click **Log a claim**. Enter the **Date**, what
is wrong in **What is wrong?**, **What was done**, and the **Parts cost** and
**Labour cost**. Click **Save**. The app shows whether the unit was in warranty
on that date.

### 14.6 Installations

Record what was fitted, where, when and by whom — even for customers who never
buy a maintenance contract.

1. Go to **Service → Installations**.
2. Click **New installation**.
3. Choose the **Customer**, the **Item name**, the **Installed on** date, the
   **Location**, the **Engineer** and any **Notes**.
4. Click **Save**.

Use **Edit** and **Delete** from the row menu to correct or remove a record. The
**Serial number** column shows the unit's serial where there is one.

## 15. Kits, barcodes and labels

### 15.1 Building a kit

1. Go to **Inventory → Kits & bundles**.
2. Click **New kit**.
3. Enter the **Name**, the **Item code**, the **Kit price** and the
   **VAT treatment**.
4. Under **Parts**, click **Add** once for each part, pick the item and set the

5. Click **Save**.

The list shows **Ready to build**, or how many parts are short on stock. You can

### 15.2 Printing barcode labels

1. Go to **Inventory → Barcode labels**.
2. Search or filter to find what you need.
3. Tick each item — ticking the **Products** box on the toolbar selects
   everything on that page.
4. Click **Print labels**.

Three labels print per sheet of A4. Cut the sheet up and stick them on the

### 15.3 Importing your product list from a spreadsheet

If you already have your items in Excel or CSV:

1. Go to **Administration → Import products**.
2. Click **Choose file** and pick the spreadsheet. It needs columns for name,
   quantity and cost.
3. The app checks the file first. You see **rows ready** and
   **rows with problems**, and every problem row is listed underneath so you can
   fix the file.
4. Leave **Update products that already exist** ticked if items with a matching
   **Item code** should be updated; untick it to leave existing items alone.
5. Click **Import items**. The button shows how many rows it will write.

Nothing is written until you press **Import items**, so a bad file cannot damage
your catalogue.

## 16. Backing up

### 16.1 Why it matters

Everything lives in one file on this computer. A backup is a copy of that file
somewhere else. Without a copy somewhere else, a failed disk means no data.

### 16.2 How to back up

The app backs up by itself:

- **Once a day**, on the first time you open it.
- **Every time you close it properly.**

Click the **X** to close rather than pulling the plug. Only a proper close makes
the backup.

### 16.3 Where backups go

Out of the box they land in the app's own folder on this computer. That protects
you against a mistake, not against a failed disk.

**Set the folder to a USB stick or a OneDrive folder:**

1. Go to **Administration → Backup & restore**.
2. Click **Change folder**.
3. Pick your USB stick or your OneDrive folder.
4. Every backup from now on goes there.

> Plug the USB stick in **before** you choose it. Windows will not let you pick
> a drive that is not connected.

A OneDrive folder is a good choice because it is already being copied somewhere
else. A USB stick in a different drawer is a good choice too. **A folder on the
network is not** — ask your supplier about that.

### 16.4 How many are kept

The **Keep last N backups** box on the same screen decides this. It is set to
30 by default, which is about a month. When the folder holds more than that, the
oldest are removed. Ten is plenty for a small shop if you also keep a weekly USB
copy.

The screen shows **Last backup** and **Backups saved**. Click **Back up now**
once and check that both change — that is how you know backups are really
running.

### 16.5 Backing up now

Click **Back up now** on the **Backup & restore** screen. Do this:

- before you hand the computer to someone else,
- before you import a large spreadsheet,
- at the end of a busy day.

The files are named so you can tell them apart, with the date and time:

| Name starts with | It was taken |
| --- | --- |
| `auto-` | On the first opening of the day |
| `onclose-` | When you closed the app |
| `backup-` | Before the app upgraded itself |

### 16.6 Restoring — ask an administrator

> **A restore replaces everything currently in the app.** Anything recorded since
> that backup was taken will be gone. The app itself warns:
> *This replaces all current data with the backup's contents.*

Ask an administrator if you think you need this. They will take a backup of the
current data first, check the file is sound, then type **RESTORE** to confirm.
The app then restarts itself. Do not close it while that is happening.

## 17. Printing

Everything you can print is printed from the screen it belongs to, with the
**Print** button. Nothing is sent anywhere; the printer is your own.

| Document | Where you print it |
| --- | --- |
| Invoice, quotation, purchase, credit note | The document screen, **Print**. **Save as PDF** also saves a copy. |
| Customer or supplier statement | **Customers** or **Suppliers** → open the customer → **Statement** → **Print** |
| Payment receipt | **Payments** → **Print** on the row, or the receipt prints by itself when you save |
| Partner closing statement | **Partners** → **Print** |
| Service reminder | **Filter schedule** → **Print reminder** |
| Barcode labels | **Barcode labels** → **Print labels** |
| VAT report | **VAT report** → **Print** |

### 17.1 What is printed on every document

- Your **company name**, **TRN**, address, phone and **logo** — set these in
  **Administration → Settings → Company details**.
- The document number, date, due date and the customer's details.
- Each line with quantity, unit price, VAT treatment, VAT and total.
- The **Subtotal**, **VAT** and **Total**.
- The **Footer text** and **Terms and conditions** from
  **Administration → Settings → Invoice & receipt**.

### 17.2 Print options

In **Administration → Settings → Invoice & receipt** you can set the
**Footer text**, the **Terms and conditions**, the **Financial year** and a
switch labelled **Print receipts on 80mm thermal paper** for shops with a receipt
printer at the counter.

### 17.3 If the printer does not print

1. Check the printer is switched on and has paper.
2. Check it is set as the default printer in Windows.
3. Try **Save as PDF** instead, then print the PDF by opening it.
4. Try a different printer if you have more than one.

If nothing prints, tell your supplier. Nothing is lost — the document is saved
either way.

## 18. Using Arabic

1. Click the **language** button in the top bar — the globe icon.

You can also change it in **Administration → Settings → Appearance**, or from

Your choice is remembered.

The whole screen flips. The text runs from right to left, the menu moves to the

## 19. If something goes wrong

### 19.1 Wrong username or password

The app says **Wrong username or password.** It does not say which one was
wrong. Check the **Username** carefully — capitals and spaces matter.

### 19.2 Your account is locked

After **5** wrong attempts in a row the account locks for **15 minutes**. Wait
15 minutes and sign in again. If you still cannot get in, an administrator can
reset your password from **Administration → Users**.

### 19.3 "This period is locked"

Someone has locked that month. Nothing can be added, changed or removed in a
locked period. Ask an administrator to open it for you with **Unlock period** on
**Finance → Period lock**, or to record the entry in the current month instead.

### 19.4 "Not enough stock"

You are trying to sell or issue more of an item than you have. The message tells
you what is **On hand** and how much was needed.

- Check you picked the right item.
- Record a **New purchase** if you have just bought more.
- Correct the count with **Set stock** if the shelf is right and the app is
  wrong.
- Reduce the **Quantity** on the line if the customer is taking fewer.

The same thing happens with a kit when one of its parts is short.

### 19.5 A number looks wrong

Do not correct it by hand. Check, in this order:

1. Did you record the purchase? Missing purchases make profit look too high.
2. Did you enter the right cost on the purchase?
3. Did you pick the right customer? An invoice must have a customer.
4. Is the period you are looking at locked?

If it still looks wrong, tell your supplier and give them the document number.

### 19.6 The printer does not print

See [17.3 If the printer does not print](#173-if-the-printer-does-not-print).

### 19.7 Nothing happens when you click something

The item may be outside your role. If you see
**You do not have access to this screen.**, ask an administrator to change your
role.

### 19.8 A screen shows **Something went wrong**

If a screen fails, a message appears saying **Something went wrong** with a
**Restart the screen** button. Your data has not been changed, and a
diagnostics file has been written to your Desktop. Press **Restart the screen**,
and if it happens again tell your supplier and send them that file.

### 19.9 You deleted something by mistake

If a **Deleted.** message appears with an **Undo** button, press **Undo**
immediately. It stays for a few seconds. If it has gone, the **Undo** is no
longer offered and you will see *That action cannot be undone.*

Note what has already moved stock or money — issuing an invoice, taking a
payment — is never given an **Undo**. For those, use **Cancel document**.

### 19.10 Asking for help

- Tell your supplier:

  - the exact wording of any message,
  - the document or invoice number,
  - and attach the diagnostics file from
    **Administration → Backup & restore → Export diagnostics**.
